# Direction round notes

## Product scene

In an Advanced Databases class demo, the student operates product CRUD while the instructor reviews search, stock visibility, and the MongoDB document model. The product-specific mechanism is that one product record is both editable catalogue content and an inspectable MongoDB document. The first screen must keep the record index, edit controls, and nested document legible together.

The category norm is a white CRUD table with a side form. Its predictable opposite is a one-item-at-a-time product feed; neither belongs in the grounded candidate list.

## Grounded candidates, ordered by resonance

1. Database inspection workbench: direct to the tool context and document model; can keep edits and JSON together.
2. University library accession index: familiar search and classification system for both students and instructors.
3. Practical lab notebook: connects the assignment setting to structured records and repeatable editing.
4. Course mark sheet: strong row scanning for an instructor, but risks implying assessment data.
5. Retail shelf ticket and stock sheet: familiar prices and availability, but weaker on MongoDB structure.
6. JSON tree and schema map: explains nested fields, but weak for product browsing and editing alone.
7. Museum accession register: diverse products behave like individually inspectable records with category-specific specs; can carry index, editor, and document view in one system.

The direction roll assigns candidate 7, Specimen Register. Candidate 1 is the grounded pick, Document Workbench.
